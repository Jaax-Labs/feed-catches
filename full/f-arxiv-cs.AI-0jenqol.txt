Computer Science > Artificial Intelligence arXiv:2611.03121v1 (cs) [Submitted on 2 Nov 2026] Title: ComplianceAtlas: A Taxonomy-Driven Agentic AI Framework for Automated AI Act Gap Analysis and Remediation Authors: Ilias Driouich , Hongliu Cao , Eoin Thomas , Yannick Devaux , Agnes Brodelle View a PDF of the paper titled ComplianceAtlas: A Taxonomy-Driven Agentic AI Framework for Automated AI Act Gap Analysis and Remediation, by Ilias Driouich and 4 other authors View PDF HTML (experimental) Abstract: Assessing regulatory compliance for high-risk AI systems requires systematic identification of technical gaps such as missing controls, in- adequate processes, or shallow evaluation of practices. Compliance officers typically admin- ister structured questionnaires collecting evi- dence of current practices, where organizations respond with descriptions of their existing pro- cesses, technical capabilities, and architectural choices. However, translating these compli- ance questionnaire responses into typed, ac- tionable gap diagnoses remains unsystematic. In this paper, we present a literature-grounded taxonomy of technical compliance gaps cover- ing areas such as accuracy, robustness, logging, and human oversight, and embed it in GapTo- Compliance, a two-stage agentic framework coordinating two specialized agents: a Gap Extraction Agent that performs hierarchical rea- soning from compliance areas to specific gap types, and a Remediation Planning Agent that ranks remediation actions from a remediation catalog via multi-signal scoring combining se- mantic similarity, regulatory alignment, and effort estimation. We evaluate this framework against four automatic compliance gap detec- tion baselines across 9 AI systems and 6 AI families. Our approach achieves a gap-type F1 of 0.831 (vs. 0.533 for the strongest baseline) and remediation P@5 of 0.872 (vs. 0.635 for retrieval methods), demonstrating promising potential for automated compliance workflows.
